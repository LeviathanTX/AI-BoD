# Jason Clawcanis's Tactical Advice Patterns

Specific, actionable recommendations based on Jason Calacanis's advisory work and teaching.

## Fundraising Tactics

### The Perfect Pitch:

**One-Sentence Pitch Structure:**
"We're [what you do] for [target market], and we're different because [unique advantage]."

**Example:**
- "We're Uber for dog walking" (bad - not differentiated)
- "We're building software that helps veterinarians diagnose diseases 10x faster using AI" (better)

**10-Slide Pitch Deck:**
1. **Cover**: Company name, logo, tagline
2. **Problem**: What sucks today? Make it painful and real
3. **Solution**: Your product in one clear sentence
4. **Why Now**: What changed to make this possible?
5. **Market Size**: TAM, SAM, SOM (be realistic)
6. **Competition**: Who else is trying to solve this?
7. **Product**: Show, don't tell (screenshots, demo)
8. **Business Model**: How do you make money?
9. **Team**: Why you? What's your unfair advantage?
10. **The Ask**: How much, what for, what milestones?

### Investor Outreach Strategy:

**The Email Template:**
```
Subject: [Mutual Connection] suggested I reach out

Hi [First Name],

[Mutual Connection] suggested I reach out. We're building [one sentence pitch].

We've got [impressive traction metric] and are raising [$X] to [specific goal].

Would love 15 minutes to get your feedback. Available [Day 1] or [Day 2]?

Best,
[Your Name]
[Title]
[Product.com]
```

**Key Rules:**
- Always get warm intro, never cold email
- One paragraph maximum
- Specific ask with specific dates
- Make it easy to say yes
- Show traction upfront

### Fundraising Timeline:

**Week 1-2: Preparation**
- Finalize pitch deck
- Practice pitch 50 times
- Prepare data room
- Get warm intros lined up

**Week 3-6: Pitching**
- 20-30 investor meetings
- Fast follow-ups within 24 hours
- Create FOMO with momentum
- Update investors on progress

**Week 7-8: Closing**
- Narrow to 2-3 interested investors
- Negotiate term sheets
- Pick the best partner, not highest valuation
- Close fast before momentum dies

## Product Development Tactics

### Launch Strategy:

**The "Do Things That Don't Scale" Approach:**
1. **Manual Everything**: No automation until you have 100 customers
2. **White Glove Service**: Personally onboard first customers
3. **Iterate Daily**: Ship updates based on feedback constantly
4. **Talk to Every Customer**: Personally, not through surveys
5. **Fix Bugs Immediately**: Respond to issues within hours

**Launch Readiness Checklist:**
- [ ] Does it solve the core problem?
- [ ] Can someone use it without your help?
- [ ] Have 10 people used it successfully?
- [ ] Is the core experience good enough?
- [ ] Can you handle 10x the users?

### MVP Definition:

**Minimum Viable Product:**
"The simplest version that delivers the core value proposition. If you're not embarrassed when you launch, you waited too long."

**What to Include:**
- Core functionality that solves the main problem
- Basic UI that's functional, doesn't need to be beautiful
- Payment system (if you're charging)

**What to Exclude:**
- Nice-to-have features
- Perfect design and branding
- Scalable infrastructure (initially)
- Comprehensive analytics
- Admin dashboards and tools

## Customer Acquisition Tactics

### The "First 100 Customers" Playbook:

**Weeks 1-4: Friends and Family (0-10 customers)**
- Personal network outreach
- Ask everyone you know to try it
- Offer for free, just need feedback
- Iterate based on their usage

**Weeks 5-8: Strangers Who Care (10-50 customers)**
- Reddit, Twitter, Product Hunt
- Niche communities and forums
- Direct outreach to target personas
- Still mostly free or heavy discount

**Weeks 9-12: Paying Customers (50-100 customers)**
- Start charging (even just $1)
- Validate willingness to pay
- Referral programs and invites
- Double down on channels that work

### Growth Channel Prioritization:

**Test in This Order:**
1. **Direct Sales**: Founder manually selling (B2B)
2. **Content Marketing**: SEO and organic traffic
3. **Community Building**: Reddit, Twitter, niche forums
4. **Paid Social**: Facebook, Instagram ads (consumer)
5. **Partnerships**: Integration and co-marketing
6. **PR**: Only after you have traction

**Channel Evaluation:**
- Can you get 100 customers this way?
- What's the CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)?
- Is it repeatable and scalable?
- How long is the sales cycle?
- What's the payback period?

## Hiring and Team Building

### First 10 Hires:

**Hire in This Order:**
1. **Technical Co-founder** (if you're non-technical)
2. **Engineer #2** (to help CTO ship faster)
3. **Designer** (make product beautiful and usable)
4. **Customer Success** (handle support and feedback)
5. **Sales/BD** (if B2B, start selling)
6. **Engineer #3** (keep shipping features)
7. **Marketing** (scale what's working)
8. **Operations** (handle logistics and processes)
9. **Engineer #4** (technical debt paydown)
10. **Finance/Admin** (bookkeeping and HR)

**Hiring Criteria:**
- **Hustle**: Do they go above and beyond?
- **Smart**: Can they learn and adapt quickly?
- **Gets Shit Done**: Track record of execution?
- **Cultural Fit**: Will they thrive in chaos?
- **Passionate**: Do they care about the mission?

### Interview Process:

**For Every Hire:**
1. **Phone Screen** (30 min): Culture fit and basic qualifications
2. **In-Person #1** (1 hour): Technical or role-specific assessment
3. **Trial Project** (2-4 hours paid): Actual work simulation
4. **Team Interview** (1 hour): Meet potential teammates
5. **Reference Checks**: Talk to 3-5 previous colleagues
6. **Founder Meeting**: Final sell and culture alignment

## Operating Metrics and KPIs

### What to Track (Early Stage):

**Core Metrics:**
- **Revenue**: Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) or GMV
- **Growth Rate**: Week-over-week or month-over-month growth
- **Burn Rate**: Monthly cash burn
- **Runway**: Months of cash remaining
- **CAC**: Customer acquisition cost
- **LTV**: Customer lifetime value
- **Churn**: Monthly customer or revenue churn

**Dashboard Update Frequency:**
- Daily: Revenue, signups, active users
- Weekly: Growth rate, burn rate, unit economics
- Monthly: Full financial statements, board metrics

### When to Worry:

**Red Flags:**
- Growth rate slowing for 2+ months
- CAC rising faster than LTV
- Churn increasing above 5% monthly
- Runway below 6 months
- Team turnover above 10% quarterly

## Crisis Management

### The "Shit Hit the Fan" Protocol:

**When Something Goes Very Wrong:**

1. **Assess Damage** (Hour 1)
   - How bad is it really?
   - Who's affected?
   - Is it existential?

2. **Communicate** (Hour 2-6)
   - Tell team immediately and honestly
   - Inform board/investors same day
   - Customers need to know (if affects them)
   - Prepare public statement if needed

3. **Fix It** (Day 1-7)
   - All hands on deck to solve
   - Work 24/7 if necessary
   - Bring in help if needed
   - Keep everyone updated on progress

4. **Learn** (Week 2)
   - What went wrong and why?
   - How do we prevent this again?
   - What systems need to change?
   - Document lessons learned

### Common Crises and Responses:

**Co-Founder Breakup:**
- Mediate first, separate if irreconcilable
- Move fast (within weeks, not months)
- One founder stays, other leaves cleanly
- Legal docs for equity buyback
- Communicate to team and investors

**Ran Out of Money:**
- Evaluate: Pivot, sell, or shut down?
- Bridge financing from existing investors
- Emergency fundraise (30-day sprint)
- Cut burn rate by 50% immediately
- Consider acqui-hire if no path forward

**Key Employee Quit:**
- Exit interview to understand why
- Assess impact on team and product
- Plan for knowledge transfer
- Consider counter-offer only if retention issue
- Promote from within or hire quickly

**Product Failure/Outage:**
- Fix it immediately (all hands)
- Communicate status every hour
- Compensate affected customers
- Post-mortem within 48 hours
- Implement prevention measures

## Competitor Response Strategy

### When Competitor Raises Money:

**Don't Panic:**
- Money doesn't guarantee success
- Execution matters more than funding
- Focus on your customers, not competitors

**Do This:**
- Double down on your differentiation
- Talk to customers about their choice
- Accelerate product development
- Consider raising more yourself
- Stay focused on your roadmap

### When Competitor Launches Similar Feature:

**Assess:**
- Is it actually better than yours?
- Do customers care?
- Can you leapfrog them?

**Respond:**
- Build it better, not just different
- Ship updates faster than they can
- Focus on customer experience, not feature parity
- Communicate your advantage clearly

## Tactical Advice by Situation

### "We're not growing fast enough"

**Diagnose First:**
- Is product problem (retention) or acquisition problem (growth)?
- Check metrics: Are new users engaging?
- Talk to churned customers: Why did they leave?

**If Acquisition Problem:**
- 10x your outbound efforts
- Test 5 new channels this month
- Increase marketing spend 2x
- Consider paid acquisition

**If Retention Problem:**
- Stop acquiring new users temporarily
- Fix the product first
- Talk to power users about what's working
- Remove features that confuse users

### "Should we pivot?"

**Pivot Criteria:**
- Core metrics haven't improved in 6+ months
- Market feedback consistently says "no"
- Team has lost conviction
- Better opportunity discovered

**Don't Pivot Because:**
- It's hard (it's always hard)
- You're bored
- Competitor launched something
- One investor suggested it

### "How do we get press?"

**Jason's PR Strategy:**
- Product Hunt launch (build community first)
- Exclusive to one tech blog (TechCrunch, The Verge)
- Founder Twitter presence and engagement
- Podcast circuit (niche podcasts first)
- Controversy and hot takes (carefully)

**Don't:**
- Hire expensive PR firm early stage
- Blast press release to 100 outlets
- Talk to press without traction
- Expect press to drive meaningful growth
